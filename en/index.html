Why these two, and why free

Both tools exist because the headline number is usually the wrong one. A payment processor's "2.9%" becomes 32.9% on a $5 sale once the fixed fee is counted, and an investment's "7% a year" becomes about 4% once inflation is subtracted from what it can actually buy. The calculators put the honest number on the screen and show the formula behind it, so the result can be checked by hand.

They run entirely in the browser: nothing you type is sent to a server, and no account is required. Rates and tax rules are read from official published sources and dated on each page; where a rule is a proposal rather than law, the page says so.

How to use them together

  • Pricing a product: start with the fee calculator's reverse mode to find the price that nets your target, then check the margin after your product cost.
  • Deciding on a platform: compare the same $100 sale across routes — the fixed fee, not the percentage, decides small tickets.
  • Planning savings: take the net profit per sale into the FIRE calculator as a monthly contribution and see the date it moves.
  • Checking risk: rerun the FIRE calculator at 3% and 5% withdrawal rates. If the plan only works at 5%, it is a hope, not a plan.
